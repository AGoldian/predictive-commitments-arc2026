Question

We test whether recent prediction errors improve shared inference allocation beyond an existing progress-aware scheduler. A learned progress model uses a fixed representation of paired errors, with matched prediction interface, observations, probability/outcome moments and fitting.

We build on Franzen's ARC-AGI-3 Milestone 2 solution and Tufa Labs' Duck harness [1,2]. Earlier work checks predictions against observations [3,4]; a public plan proposes calibration-conditioned priority [5]. Prediction checking, shared scheduling and multi-agent review are not our novelty claims. We test the incremental paired-error representation under fixed regularization.

Method

Before a real action, the model may supply probabilities for frame change, level completion and attempt failure. Omission is permitted abstention. A host binds the prediction or abstention to the action/payload and scores valid predictions once against permitted returned observations. RESET/undo remain separate; refused actions have no executed target and unavailable labels remain missing. Solver logic excludes environment implementations, hidden state, future answers and game-specific solution rules.

For each event, the history retains the last eight eligible actions and computes mean squared prediction error only over valid observable pairs. Common features include progress, accepted target tokens, action history, observation novelty, prediction availability and mean probability, squared probability and outcome on that same pair set.

A1 uses these common features; P adds three event Brier means. Both logistic models predict current-level completion before 8,192 additional target-output tokens or 20 nonautomatic actions. Censored windows remain unavailable; observational labels do not identify causal computation value. Identical completed rows, development standardization, missingness rules, equal weights, L2 coefficient 1.0 and 2,000 full-gradient steps train both models. The frozen progress probability multiplies baseline priority by 0.5 plus that probability; capacity is two request/tool exchanges. P uses A1 when all event residuals are missing and reports fallback frequency.

B0 retains baseline prompts/batch policy under common accounting/admission. B1 uses the prediction interface and baseline priority. P versus A1 is the mechanism contrast; B1 versus B0 describes total instrumentation, and P versus B0 the complete method under matched limits.

Why the representation controls matter

Brier equals mean(p-squared)+mean(y)−2mean(p-times-y), revealing pairing beyond common marginals. The saved constant-q diagnostics replaced common forecast moments and fitted both A1_q/P_q on derivative rows; their matrix differs from original A1. Added Brier training columns are zero at q=0.5 and signed outcome duplicates at q=0.25/0.75 when training variance is positive. Duplication halves that coordinate's effective L2 penalty and doubles its gradient step. Differences compare raw training probabilities within each derivative pair, supplying no new pairing information or policy evidence.
