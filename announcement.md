# Testing Predictive Commitments in Interactive Reasoning

**Alexander Serov — Independent Researcher**  
Research release, 29 October 2026

This paper presents an implemented ARC-AGI-3 method and a negative feasibility result. It asks whether paired action-prediction errors help allocate shared inference effort beyond matched probability/outcome features.

In the four-arm development pilot, P supplied valid forecasts for **89 of 417 eligible actions (21.3429%)**, below the prespecified **50%** requirement. B1 and A1 met that requirement, but their results cannot replace P's failed condition. **No eligible P−A1 efficacy comparison is available.**

The corrected main-audit B0 run passed all 21 evidence checks and retained all ten public games: one won and nine recorded crashed. Its descriptive observed-public-prefix mean was **14.491341991341992**. The following B1 passed 19 of 21 checks but remained ineligible; no B1 score was calculated. Main-audit A1/P and the other eight fleets were not launched. The earlier main refusal, failed held-out calibration and failed first full published-policy reproduction remain reported.

The separate full instrumented Bpub run also ended ERROR. Its original strict readout retains all 100 planned slots as evidence_unavailable, with both full aggregates and upstream reproduction unavailable. Two sandbox deadlines triggered a shared fail-closed stop before the whole-work deadline; the cause of snippet delay is unestablished. The cost scan's zero known lower bound is not measured zero expenditure. Neither instrumented attempt establishes the unmodified upstream policy's outcome.

The corresponding official P submission received **public score 0.00**. Its rerun traces and resource use are unavailable, and the cause is unexplained. These observations do not establish allocator benefit, verification necessity or generalization to hidden games.
